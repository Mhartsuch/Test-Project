Por-Rok in a Glass Door Rail

Setting heavy tempered glass into a wet-glaze entrance door rail with Por-Rok anchoring cement — product data, mix, sequence, and the failure modes that matter.

POR-ROK® / SUPER POR-ROK®
SpecChem · CGM Building Products
Non-shrink hydraulic anchoring cement
Rev. 2026-07

Sequence — glass into rail

  1. Confirm the glass is final. Fully tempered, cut, edged, and holes drilled before glazing. Nothing can be worked after tempering. Reject chipped or shelled edges — a nick under cement becomes a crack under load.
  2. Match rail to glass thickness. Wet-glaze rails are made for 3/8", 1/2", 5/8", and 3/4" glass. Dry-fit the lite in the channel and confirm the engagement depth the rail maker specifies before any water hits powder.
  3. Clean and mask. Degrease the channel — no oil, dust, or chips. Mask the outside faces of the rail and the glass an inch above the rail line. Cured cement does not come off anodizing or glass without damage.
  4. Set on blocks. Neoprene or silicone setting blocks, 85 ±5 Shore A, minimum 4" long (0.1" of block per ft² of glass), at the quarter points, supporting the full glass thickness.
  5. Center and shim. Equal cement space on both faces. Shim with rubber wedges, then check the lite for square and plumb and the rail for level. Brace it — this is the position it cures in.
  6. Dam every opening. Tape or clay the rail ends, lock cutouts, pivot pockets, and closer cavities. Cement that runs into a closer pocket is a rail you cut off.
  7. Mix by measure. Water in the bucket first, then powder: 16 oz water per 5 lb. Mix ~2 min to a lump-free flowable consistency. Batch only what you can place in about 10 minutes. No sand, no additives, no retempering.
  8. Pour and rod. One continuous pass, both sides of the glass, working end to end. Rod with a thin strip of stock to release voids — a void at the block line is a stress point. Fill to the rail maker's line, not the lip.
  9. Clean it green. Strike off squeeze-out and sponge the glass and rail faces while the mix is still soft; pull masking before initial set. You have roughly 10–15 minutes of that window.
  10. Let it stand, then hang. Initial set 20–30 min; do not bump or shift the assembly. Trade practice is to leave the door overnight before hanging, and never torque pivots, patches, or closer hardware into green cement.

Stop — the five that cause callbacks

  • Wrong product for an exterior door. Por-Rok is gypsum-based and loses strength if left exposed to water — interior only. Exterior or wet-exposure work calls for Super Por-Rok or another gypsum-free hydraulic cement.
  • Super Por-Rok against bare aluminum. The maker's own guidance: use Por-Rok for aluminum rails; if Super Por-Rok must be used, the aluminum must be coated inside and out, or uncontrolled expansion splits the rail.
  • Retempering a stiffening batch. Adding water after the mix starts to go kills strength. Dump it and mix fresh.
  • Glazing a rail the manufacturer specifies as dry-glaze. Taper-Loc and dry-glaze rails are not cement rails — cementing them voids the system.
  • Cement left to cure on glass or finish. Cured is permanent. Clean while green, every time.

How much for one rail

in³ = L × D × (W‑chan − t‑glass)
lb ≈ in³ ÷ 17

Worked: a 36" rail, 3.5" fill depth, 3/4" channel on 1/2" glass → 36 × 3.5 × 0.25 = 31.5 in³ ≈ 2 lb. Two rails on a door, plus waste: a 10 lb pail covers it.

Derived from the published 0.5 ft³ / 50 lb yield (864 in³ ÷ 50 = 17.3 in³ per lb). Estimate up, not down — a short pour cannot be topped off after set.

If it's an exterior door

Rail-system specs for wet glazing typically call for a gypsum-free hydraulic cement rated for exterior use, or epoxy, exceeding 1,500 psi @ 24 hr and 4,000 psi @ 28 days. Standard Por-Rok is gypsum-based and does not meet that; Super Por-Rok or an equivalent expanding cement does. Follow the rail manufacturer's instruction over this sheet when the two disagree.

PPE

Alkaline cement dust with crystalline silica. Dust mask, sealed gloves, eye protection. Wash cement off skin before it dries; flush eyes 15 min and seek care. Mix in ventilated space, add powder to water to keep dust down.

Scope note. The manufacturer's literature for Por-Rok covers anchoring bolts, railings, and machinery — it does not publish a glazing procedure. The product data above is from the manufacturer; the sequence is trade practice for wet-glaze rails combined with published glazing-industry guidance on setting blocks and grout. The rail manufacturer's own installation instructions govern engagement depth, fill line, and approved cement.
