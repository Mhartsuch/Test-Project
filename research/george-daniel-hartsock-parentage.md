# The Parentage of George Daniel Hartsock (b. ~1790–1800)

**Research report — June 12, 2026**

## The question

Identify the parents of **George Daniel Hartsock**, born roughly 1790–1800, presumed USA.
Surname variants considered: Hartsock, Hartsuch, Hartsough, Hartsoch, Hartsook, Hartsoe,
Hartsuck, Hartzog, Hertzog, Herzog. The requesting family's present-day spelling is
**Hartsuch**, which constrains which descendant line is most likely relevant.

## Methodology and an important evidence caveat

Research was conducted as a fan-out of five parallel web-search investigations
(candidate enumeration; Hartsuch-spelling trace-back; early family-cluster mapping;
primary-record hunt; graves/obituaries/compiled genealogies), followed by a three-voter
adversarial verification pass on nine load-bearing claims (a claim needed independent
corroboration to be rated "confirmed" and was killed on demonstrated contradiction),
plus a targeted hunt for the modern Hartsuch line's earliest documented ancestor.
Roughly 280 searches were run in total.

> **Caveat:** this research environment's network policy blocked direct page retrieval
> (HTTP 403) from every genealogy site tested (FamilySearch, WikiTree, Findagrave, Geni,
> MyHeritage, Ancestry, Genealogy.com, RootsWeb, archive.org). **All evidence below was
> obtained from search-engine result snippets**, cross-checked across independently
> phrased queries, but not read from the full source pages. Every citation should be
> opened and confirmed by a human before being entered into a family tree. Paywalled
> record sets (Ancestry, Fold3, full FamilySearch records) were out of reach entirely.

## Headline findings

1. **No documented person named "George Daniel Hartsock" (any spelling) born 1785–1805
   exists in any indexed online source reachable from this environment.** Three
   independent verification passes failed to refute this. The name as remembered is
   therefore most likely (a) an undocumented/unindexed man — the leading candidate being
   an unrecorded son of the only pre-1850 family in America using the exact **Hartsuch**
   spelling — or (b) a conflation of two related men (a George and a Daniel), of which
   this family network offers several documented examples.

2. **Consequently, no parentage can yet be responsibly stated as fact.** The
   evidence-ranked candidate parent couples are, in order:
   - **William Hartsuch & Elizabeth (Barnett) Hartsuch** of Baltimore, married
     22 Jan 1797 — if the target carried the Hartsuch spelling from birth (Hypothesis 1);
   - **Jonathan Hartsock (1753–1817) & Hannah Guizebard/Geisbert** of Huntingdon Co.,
     PA → Fairfield Co., OH — if he hides among their ~11 unnamed children (Hypothesis 3);
   - **John Hartsock (b. ~1760) & Catherine Elizabeth Harding** of Frederick Co., MD —
     if the target is really George of Bellbrook, b. ~1781/82 (Hypothesis 4);
   - **Daniel Hartzog & Susanna Zorn** of Barnwell District, SC — parents of the only
     *verified* "George with a father named Daniel" in the window, but in a separate
     family that is geographically and onomastically a poor fit (Hypothesis 2b).

3. **The requester's own line is solid back to ~1870 and then stops:** David Hartsuch
   (b. 1961) ← Grover Paul Hartsuch (1926, Pontiac, MI – 2022, Davenport, IA) ← Paul
   Jackson Hartsuch (1902, Kendallville, IN – 1982) ← **George Wesley Hartsuch**
   (m. Agnes Isabella Ritter; b. ~1855–1880) — whose own parents appear in **no indexed
   online source**. Closing that one gap (census 1880/1900, an Indiana death
   certificate, or FamilySearch person L6G3-9LP "Clara Maude Hartsuch 1880–1951") is
   the single highest-leverage next step, and will likely determine which hypothesis
   about the 1790s generation is the right one.

4. The **Hartsuch spelling is demonstrably old in this family network**: the 1775
   Frederick Co., MD Committee of Observation list and 1777 Oaths of Fidelity render
   the Herzog-descended family as "George **Hartsuck**, John Hartsuck, William
   Hartsuck…" — the closest historical form to the modern spelling — and the 1797
   Baltimore marriage of **William Hartsuch** is the earliest exact-spelling record
   found anywhere.

## 1. No indexed "George Daniel Hartsock" (b. 1785–1805)

Across ~130 enumeration searches and three independent refutation attempts, no
database, tree, grave index, or record index reachable from this environment contains a
person named "George Daniel Hartsock" (or "George D." in any variant spelling) born
1785–1805. Findagrave likewise surfaced no George Hartsock/Hartsough/Hartsook/Hartsoe/
Hartsuch memorial born in that window. The nearest near-misses are George **Henry**
Hartzog (b. 1795 SC — whose *father* was named Daniel) and a "Hans George Hartsock"
b. ~1781 MD (rendered "George **Washington** Hartsock" in one tree). Snippet-level
searching cannot prove a universal negative — but three adversarial passes designed to
find a counterexample all failed.

## 3. The family framework: one trunk for the Hartsock/Hartsough/Hartsook cluster — but not for every variant

The Maryland–Pennsylvania–Ohio **Hartsock/Hartsough/Hartsook** lines trace to **Hans
Peter Herzog (c. 1700/04–1763)** of Raritan, Somerset Co., New Jersey (First Reformed
Church), who moved before ~1750–59 to Frederick Co., Maryland (renting 100 acres from
Lord Baltimore, 1759), with wife **Anna Margaretha Kuntze (1712–1784)** (one caveat in
the sources: the wife's surname "may be Schmidt or Kuntze"). The family book traces the
immigrant generation to **Rodenbach/Feldkirchen bei Neuwied in the Rhineland (documented
1689)**, with brothers Peter, Handrick (Henry) William, and Zachariah arriving in
NY/NJ in the early 1700s. Children baptized at Raritan include Peter II (b. 1733),
Nicholas (chr. 1735), **Hans George (chr. 1737/38)**, Henry (b. 1741), John, Engelbert
(b. ~1750), and Jonathan (b. 1753, per the compiled genealogy at Woodsboro, MD).

**Verification narrowed the old "all variants = one family" framing.** Confirmed
separate immigrant lines sharing the Herzog etymology: the **NC Hartsoe** family
(immigrant Friedrich Herzog/Hartsoe, ship *St. Mark* to Philadelphia 1738 → VA → Orange
Co., NC) and the **SC Hartzog** family (Swiss/German, Orangeburgh Township, 1750s).
A further unresolved conflict: Findagrave's bio for Henry Hartsock (memorial 140847711)
claims a 1749 Baden birth and 1753 immigration with a father *Frederick* — irreconcilable
with the Raritan account as written; one retelling's "ship Neptune, 1752" arrival is
internally impossible against the 1733–43 Raritan baptisms. Treat the immigrant
generation's details as approximate.

Notably, the **1775 Frederick Co., MD Committee of Observation list and 1777 Oaths of
Fidelity spell the family "HARTSUCK"** ("George Hartsuck, John Hartsuck, William
Hartsuck, Henry Heartsock, Nicholas Hortsook") — independently corroborated via a
separate Associators transcription (learnwebskills.com PDF). The -uck/-uch rendering is
thus an original Maryland variant of this exact family, not a later invention.

Principal clusters mapped (patriarch → places):

- **Guilford Twp., Franklin Co., PA** — George Hartsough Sr. (1737/38–1788/89); will
  names sons George and Henry. The only *primary-record* "son George" found.
- **Woodcock Valley, Huntingdon Co., PA** — Peter Hartsock II (1733–c.1800), builder of
  Fort Hartsock (1778); sons Daniel (~1759), Samuel, Jonathan, Peter Jr., Isaac, John —
  **no George documented**.
- **Huntingdon Co., PA → Bloom Twp., Fairfield Co., OH** — Jonathan Hartsock (1753–1817)
  m. Hannah Guizebard (also rendered "Geisbert"); township officer, Franklin Twp.,
  Huntingdon Co., 1800 (independent USGenWeb corroboration); buried Old Betzer
  Cemetery, Carroll, OH. Reportedly **14 children, of whom only Benjamin, Jonathan Jr.
  (b. ~1790), and Isaac (b. 2 Sep 1798, PA) are recoverable** from indexed sources.
- **Fairfield Co., OH (Brethren/"Dunkard")** — Daniel Hartsough (~1759–1834), son of
  Peter II & Sarah; see the conflation finding below.
- **Frederick/Washington Co., MD** — Nicholas Hartsock (1735–1821) m. Catherine
  Elizabeth; sons William T. (~1757), **John (~1760, m. Catherine Elizabeth Harding)** —
  claimed father of candidate #1 —, Nicholas Jr. (~1774), Peter (~1774). Also Henry
  Hartsock (1741–1812), whose family Bible was held in 1938 by Edwin E. Stoner of
  Westminster, MD; Henry's documented sons: Henry, Christopher, Abraham, Daniel.
- **Centre Co., PA** — Conrad Hartsock (separate Palatine immigrant, arr. 1750);
  children Catherine, Henry, Christian, Abraham, Daniel — **no George**.
- **Chatham Co., NC (Hartsoe)** — Friedrich Herzog/Hartsoe (~1695–1780), separate
  immigrant; no George in the window verified.
- **Virginia → Tennessee (Hartsook)** — Engelbert Hartsook (~1750–c.1795); sons
  unlisted in open sources; line continued to Washington Co., VA → Overton Co., TN.
- **East Tennessee (Knox/Sevier/Blount) Hartsocks** — could not be verified from
  open-web sources this session; needs subscription-database work.

### A demonstrated tree-conflation worth knowing about: the Fairfield Co. "two Daniels"

The widely copied profile "**Daniel Hartsock, b. 1796–1800 in Pennsylvania, m. Susana,
d. Jan 1834 Fairfield Co., OH, son of Jonathan Hartsock**" (from Mary Mae Hartsough's
book, echoed in trees) **failed verification** (votes: 1 refute / 2 unverifiable-with-
merge-suspicion). No source links any Daniel to Jonathan — Jonathan's recoverable sons
are Benjamin, Jonathan Jr., and Isaac. An independent Bremen-area Dunkard local study
instead documents: the man who died 1834 in Fairfield Co. with a wife named Susan was
the **elder Daniel Hartsough (b. ~1759 Frederick Co., MD, son of Peter Hartsock II &
Sarah)**, who m1. Elizabeth Fundenberg (1754–1813) and m2. the widow **Susan Fulcairth**
("the mother of his daughter-in-law"); his **son Daniel** (the man actually born in the
1790s) married **Catharine Fulcarth**, and *their* son Daniel (1826–1891) was the
Brethren minister. The "b. 1796–1800 + wife Susana + d. 1834" profile thus appears to
merge a father and son — a live warning for any tree in this family, and a caution
against trusting the same book's unsupported parent-links elsewhere.

## 4. The Hartsuch spelling: what is actually documented

- **Earliest exact-spelling record found: William Hartsuch m. Elizabeth Barnett,
  22 January 1797, Baltimore, MD** (WikiTree Barnett-4936; the stray "22 April"
  rendering did not recur in any re-search — resolved in favor of January; the
  underlying license series exists at the Maryland State Archives). Elizabeth was
  b. ~1773 Baltimore, daughter of Andrew Barnett, with a brother named **George
  Barnett** — a natural namesake for a son. One snippet places her later in Berkeley
  Co., VA (now WV). **No children of the couple are documented in any reachable
  source.** Note: the 1800 federal census for Baltimore *County* is not extant (city
  only), which partly explains the record gap.
- **The modern Hartsuch family** traces backward in open sources: David Hartsuch
  (b. 1961, Iowa state senator) ← Grover Paul Hartsuch (1926, Pontiac, Oakland Co., MI –
  2022, Davenport, IA; m. Mary Lou Holmes, Flint, 1948) ← Paul Jackson Hartsuch (1902,
  Kendallville, Noble Co., IN – 1982, bur. Boughton Cemetery, Kalamazoo Co., MI; PhD
  physical chemistry, editor of *Graphic Arts Monthly*) ← **George Wesley Hartsuch**
  (m. Agnes Isabella Ritter). The Paul-Jackson←George-Wesley link is confirmed by two
  independent source types (Who's Who-derived biography + Findagrave). **George Wesley
  Hartsuch himself — birth, marriage, census, death, parents — appears in no indexed
  online source** despite 30+ targeted queries. This is an indexing gap, not proof of
  absence: the records to pull are listed in §7.
- **Other Hartsuch/Hartsuck individuals found, born 1800–1880** (the spelling is
  extraordinarily rare — this is the complete reachable list): **Clara Maude Hartsuch
  (1880–1951)**, FamilySearch person L6G3-9LP — profile blocked from here, **the most
  valuable unexplored lead** (almost certainly a sibling, cousin, or sister-in-law of
  George Wesley); Martha Jane "Mattie" (McGill) **Hartsuck** (1869–1907), m. McClellan
  "Matt" Hartsuck 1890, Iowa — possible parallel branch; and (context, Hartsough
  spelling) Joseph Scott Hartsough (1854–1922) and Albert Henry Hartsough (1856–1911)
  of Nappanee, Elkhart Co., IN — the geographically closest same-generation Harts- men
  to Kendallville (~40 mi).
- Geographic pattern of the name: Baltimore, MD (1797) → northeast Indiana / southern
  Michigan (by ~1900) → Chicago-area Illinois and Davenport/Bettendorf, Iowa (20th c.).
  This matches the Hartsough concentration corridor (Indiana ~15% and Ohio ~11% of US
  Hartsoughs) and the Hartsough pioneer cluster of **Oakland Co., Michigan**
  (Christopher Hartsough, co-founder of the first permanent Oakland Co. settlement,
  Avon Twp., 17 Mar 1817; per an Ancestry tree, son of **Zachariah Herzog** — i.e., the
  third immigrant brother's line) — the same county where Grover Hartsuch was born in
  1926. The **Palmer Hartsough papers, 1794–1935** (Burton Historical Collection,
  Detroit Public Library) contain Hartsough "family genealogical material" reaching two
  generations before the hymn-writer Palmer Hartsough (b. 1844 Redford, MI, son of
  Wells & Thankful (Palmer) Hartsough) — a major offline resource for the Michigan
  branch, though no evidence ties Palmer's Baptist Wayne-Co. line to the Hartsuch
  spelling.
- Distinct-surname warning: **Hartsuff** (Gen. George Lucas Hartsuff, 1830–1874) is an
  unrelated family frequently confused with Hartsuch in indexes. MyHeritage's
  "Hartsugh" page calls Hartsuch a variant of Hartsugh; its "medieval English"
  etymology is boilerplate — the German Herzog derivation is the credible account.

## 6. Assessment: who was "George Daniel Hartsock," and who were his parents?

Ranked hypotheses, with the parentage answer each implies:

**H1 — An undocumented son of William Hartsuch & Elizabeth (Barnett) Hartsuch of
Baltimore (m. 22 Jan 1797).** *Parents: William Hartsuch & Elizabeth Barnett.*
Strongest circumstantial fit: the only pre-1850 family in America using the exact
Hartsuch spelling (and exact-spelling continuity matters when a spelling is this
rare); a marriage date that puts sons squarely in 1798–1810; the bride's brother named
George; and the documented MD → OH/IN corridor of the wider Herzog clan ending exactly
where the modern Hartsuch family surfaces (Noble Co., IN). Weakness: zero children of
the couple are documented anywhere reachable — this hypothesis currently rests on
geography, onomastics, and spelling alone. **Confidence: speculative but best.**

**H2 — "George Daniel" is a conflation, not one person.** This family network
demonstrably produces such errors (§3's two-Daniels merge). Sub-variants: (a) a memory
blending a George with the Fairfield Co. Daniels; (b) an echo of **George Henry Hartzog
(b. 1795 SC), son of Daniel** — the only verified George-with-a-father-Daniel in the
window (*parents: Daniel Hartzog & Susanna Zorn*), but a separate South Carolina family
that is geographically and onomastically incompatible with a Maryland/Midwest Hartsuch
line; (c) a George + Daniel brother pair elsewhere in the clan. **Confidence: quite
plausible mechanism; (b) unlikely to be the requester's line.**

**H3 — One of the ~11 unnamed children of Jonathan Hartsock (1753–1817).** *Parents:
Jonathan Hartsock & Hannah Guizebard (Geisbert).* Structurally attractive: his named
sons bracket the window (Jonathan Jr. b. ~1790, Isaac b. 1798 PA); the family moved
Huntingdon Co., PA → Fairfield Co., OH, on the road to the Indiana/Ohio Hartsough
concentration; descendants used the Hartsough spelling. But **no son George surfaced in
any search**, so this is an argument from the gap in his child list. **Confidence:
untested — decidable by reading the two family books and Jonathan's 1817 Fairfield Co.
estate file.**

**H4 — George of Bellbrook (b. ~1781/82 MD, d. 1862 Greene Co., OH, m. Elizabeth
Cramer), with the remembered birth decade a decade soft.** *Most likely parents per the
compiled genealogy: John Hartsock (b. ~1760) & Catherine Elizabeth Harding* — but that
link is a single-book claim contested by user trees (see §5 claim 1) and needs John's
Frederick Co. probate/deeds to settle. **Confidence: viable only if the family's
1790–1800 date is wrong.**

**H5 — A son of George Hartsough Jr.** (the man named in the 1788 Franklin Co., PA
will). George Jr.'s own family is completely untraced; a son George b. 1790s in
Franklin Co., PA is possible and would carry a documented chain Jr. ← Sr. & Judith.
**Confidence: untested.**

For the requester specifically: hypotheses H1 and H3 both terminate in the same
research move — **identify George Wesley Hartsuch's parents** (§7, steps 1–3). If his
father or grandfather proves to be a George (Daniel) Hartsuch/Hartsough born 1790–1800,
the question answers itself; the family memory of "George Daniel Hartsock b. 1790–1800"
is exactly two undocumented generations above the current dead-end, which is consistent
with how such memories typically survive (right name and era, fuzzy on the rest).

## 7. Recommended next research steps

**Tier 1 — close the Hartsuch gap (highest leverage, all subscription/login-walled
rather than nonexistent):**
1. **FamilySearch person L6G3-9LP — Clara Maude Hartsuch (1880–1951)**: open the
   profile (free login); her parents/siblings almost certainly reveal George Wesley
   Hartsuch's household.
2. **1900 federal census, Kendallville/Wayne Twp., Noble Co., IN** — George W. Hartsuch
   household: gives his birth month/year/state and parents' birthplaces; then walk back
   to his 1880/1870 boyhood household (likely under Hartsuch, Hartsough, or Hartsuck).
3. **Indiana death certificates (1899–2011)** for George Wesley and Agnes Isabella
   Hartsuch — death certificates name parents; if they died in Michigan
   (Pontiac/Flint), use Michiganology's 1921–1952 series. Also the **Noble Co.
   marriage record, Hartsuch–Ritter (~1895–1902)**, and the Noble Co. obituary indexes
   (gen.nobleco.lib.in.us; noblegenealogy.org).
4. **Full-text search the family book** *The Families Herzog… Hartsook etc.* v.1–2
   (FamilySearch Books item 367099; free login) **for "Hartsuch" and "Hartsuck"**, and
   read the John-son-of-Nicholas and Jonathan-(1753) chapters; likewise Mary Mae
   Hartsough's *History of the Hartough–Hartsough–Hartsock Family* (Allen County Public
   Library, Fort Wayne — 40 miles from Kendallville, fittingly).

**Tier 2 — test the 1790s-generation hypotheses directly:**
5. **Baltimore records for William Hartsuch**: original 1797 marriage register image
   (MD State Archives); Baltimore church baptisms 1797–1812 (First German Reformed,
